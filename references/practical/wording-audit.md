# Wording and causal scope audit

Audit what one experience sentence promises against its supplied evidence. This is
a host-led review of meaning, not a keyword blacklist, automated certification or
new decision mode. Use it instead of a less relevant practical reference; retain
the total budget of 1–3 references.

## Check the exact promise

Read the original sentence, contribution account and available sources first.
Split compound claims into personal action, ownership, project stage, result and
causal attribution. Retain source IDs / locators and confidence for each relevant
part; a checked artifact is not automatically proof of its author's identity.

- For “led” or “主导”, inspect owned product decisions, alternatives, decision
  authority and work actually done. Supported product leadership should remain;
  teammates implementing the service does not negate it. Narrow the object to the
  supported responsibility rather than replacing all leadership with participation.
- For “participated” or “参与”, preserve the actual contribution boundary. Do not
  upgrade it to leadership or personal implementation to sound impressive.
- For “delivered”, “built”, “launched” and equivalent phrases, check what was
  completed, by whom and where. Distinguish the project, this claim and the user's
  contribution stages; a completed evaluation is not a completed production system.
- For result and causal verbs such as “improved”, “drove” or “促成”, separate the
  user's action from the observed outcome and from evidence that links them.
  Before/after observations alone do not establish causality. Keep measured change
  with its sample, baseline, period and conditions when comparable; if those changed,
  report separate measurements rather than a demonstrated improvement.

Check numbers, units, denominators and percentage versus percentage-point changes.
Offline results do not prove production use or user impact. Remove unsupported
precision, not genuine evidence; plans stay planned. The same verb can be justified
in one sentence and unsupported in another. Safe wording must preserve these
boundaries inside the sentence, not rely on a distant disclaimer to repair it.

## Deliver wording-audit.md

Give the result in chat; write a file only when requested in the user's chosen
directory. For each material claim, show:

| Original phrase | Minimum usable edit | Evidence and remaining gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quote the user's exact wording | Keep it if supported; otherwise narrow only the unsupported promise | Cite the precise source and what it does / does not establish |

Then provide one consolidated usable sentence and the most consequential remaining
unknown. Ask the smallest question that would change wording when necessary; an
unknown does not require withholding an already supported part. If evidence is only
self-report, attribute it and do not describe it as independently verified.

Do not provide an unsubstantiated stronger sentence as a default alternate to copy.
Explain the missing evidence in plain language instead. If the user explicitly asks
what could justify a stronger claim, state the conditions as unmet, not completed
accomplishments. Stop expanding the claim when evidence runs out; revisit on a new
source, corrected contribution or genuinely comparable result. This review does not
predict hiring success or prove that wording caused an interview outcome.
