# Research and application index

Verified against the linked sources on 2026-10-20. This is a small working
index, not a comprehensive review or a claim that Career Junshi is effective.
Research concerns group results in specific settings; candidate evidence concerns
what this person actually did. Neither substitutes for the other.

## Read only when needed

- Use for disputed advice, requests for research, or deciding whether a proposed
  practice has relevant evidence. Ordinary rewriting does not need a literature lecture.
- Keep the total at 1–3 references: replace a less relevant general reference
  with this index; retain the practical guide needed to perform the task.
- Reopen the primary source for new quantitative claims or details beyond the
  reading depth below. Confirm title, authors, year, version and relevant passage.
- Seek current local sources for hiring demand, employer rules or regional
  conditions. These studies do not establish today's market conditions.
- If access is incomplete, state what was read and leave unsupported details
  unknown. Do not silently substitute a similarly titled chapter or later version.
- Describe design and relevance rather than assigning a universal evidence score.
  A meta-analysis of associations does not by itself establish causality.

## R1 — Job-search interventions

- **Source:** Songqi Liu, Jason L. Huang, Mo Wang (2014), *Effectiveness of
  job search interventions: A meta-analytic review*. Psychological Bulletin,
  140(4), 1009–1041; online first March 3, 2014.
  [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24588365/),
  [DOI 10.1037/a0035923](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035923).
- **Design / population:** Meta-analysis of 47 experimentally or
  quasi-experimentally evaluated interventions with job seekers.
  Country distribution and detailed eligibility were not verified from the abstract.
- **Read:** Bibliographic record and full abstract; not the complete paper,
  constituent trials or data. The abstract reports pooled employment odds of 2.67
  relative to controls and component/moderator findings.
- **Can inform:** Combine concrete job-search skill practice with feasible
  action support; do not assume encouragement alone is an adequate intervention.
- **Cannot establish:** An isolated component's causal effect, an individual's
  hiring probability, or Career Junshi's benefit. Odds ratios are not probability
  ratios; 2.67 is not a personal multiplier or a product promise.
- **Product adaptation:** Pair a small rehearsal with a manageable next action.
  This implementation has not been evaluated by this study.

## R2 — Search behavior and employment outcomes

- **Source:** Edwin A. J. van Hooft, John D. Kammeyer-Mueller, Connie R. Wanberg,
  Ruth Kanfer, Gokce Basbug (2021), *Job search and employment success:
  A quantitative review and future research agenda*. Journal of Applied
  Psychology, 106(5), 674–713; online first July 13, 2020.
  [Author-university PDF](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/sites/carlsonschool.umn.edu/files/2021-06/VanHooftKammeyerMuellerWanbergKanferBasbug_JAP%202021.pdf),
  [DOI 10.1037/apl0000675](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000675).
- **Design / population:** Quantitative synthesis of 378 independent samples
  (165,933 people), including entrants, unemployed and employed job seekers.
  Full country composition was not checked for this index.
- **Read:** Abstract, inclusion/sample description, Table 2 and related results,
  and discussion of search intensity versus quality; not every analysis or dataset.
- **Can inform:** Track application activity separately from interviews, offers
  and employment quality. Associations with more activity do not imply a better
  job; search quality is a distinct construct with fewer available studies.
- **Cannot establish:** That increasing a specific user's application count
  causes success, an optimal quota, or a causal diagnosis from a short funnel.
- **Product adaptation:** Review targeting, material quality and constraints
  before recommending more volume. This review protocol is not a tested intervention.

## R3 — Resume writing assistance

- **Source:** Emma van Inwegen, Zanele Munyikwa, John J. Horton (2023),
  *Algorithmic Writing Assistance on Jobseekers' Resumes Increases Hires*.
  [arXiv:2301.08083v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08083v1), submitted January 19,
  2023; the PDF title page is dated January 20. Preprint version pinned here.
- **Design / population:** Randomized field experiment in an online labor market:
  480,948 initially allocated entrants; 194,700 approved entrants with nonempty
  resumes in the experimental analysis. The sample spans countries; about 14%
  were US-based. A complete country list was not checked.
- **Read:** Abstract, intervention/randomization description, sample flow and
  geography in Section 2.5/Table 1, and hiring-results discussion; no replication.
- **Can inform:** Improve clarity and writing errors while preserving facts.
  The reported hiring increase is about 8% relative to controls in that setting,
  not eight percentage points.
- **Cannot establish:** Gains from LaTeX, a particular layout, generative
  embellishment, Career Junshi, or all occupations, countries and hiring systems.
- **Product adaptation:** Separate fact approval from copy editing and PDF
  presentation. That export workflow has not been tested by this experiment.

## R4 — Turning intentions into action

- **Source:** Peter M. Gollwitzer, Paschal Sheeran (2006), *Implementation
  intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes*.
  Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 38, 69–119.
  [Author-university record and PDF](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes),
  [DOI 10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1).
- **Design / population:** Meta-analysis of 94 independent tests of implementation
  intentions across goal domains. Population and country composition were not
  verified in the inspected pages; this is not direct job-search evidence.
- **Read:** University record and visually inspected scanned PDF pages 69–70,
  including the abstract and introduction; not the full methods or trial reports.
- **Can inform:** Link a specific anticipated cue to a feasible response.
- **Cannot establish:** A guaranteed habit, a hiring effect, or that a particular
  schedule works for this user. General goal research cannot validate career advice.
- **Product adaptation:** Offer one optional if–then action, with an obstacle
  fallback and review point. The fallback/review protocol is an engineering choice.

## Compact basis card

When the basis matters, use a short card in the conversation; no automatic file:

`Advice | Source ID + link/version | Design + population | Read depth |
Relevant finding | Transfer limit | Product adaptation | Next observation`

Keep user facts and missing evidence separate from this card. Label an unsupported
practice as an engineering hypothesis, not a research finding. Ask what observation
would change the advice; local feedback can guide iteration without proving cause.
Do not infer career fit from attachment styles, personality labels or unrelated
relationship research. Tests of packaging or synthetic conversations establish
software/workflow behavior, not improved real hiring outcomes.
